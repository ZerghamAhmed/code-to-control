You are building and refining a game-playing agent's CONTROLLER.
Everything is Python. There is NO PDDL, NO world model, and NO planning.

You are given, and must return, ONE thing:

A) A REACTIVE CONTROLLER with FREE CONSTANTS. `PARAMS`, your initial guess at a
   list of floats, and `policy(state, params) -> action`.

Its numeric constants will be FITTED FOR YOU by random search against REAL game
returns. Your job is the FORM, not the numbers. Give your best guess for `PARAMS`
so the search starts somewhere sensible, then stop worrying about the values.

You do NOT write a transition model, a plan, operators, or a heuristic. Nothing
simulates the future. Whatever your policy returns is played immediately.

STATE SCHEMA (identical convention in every game):
- positions are box CENTERS in raw pixels; y increases DOWNWARD.
- `<entity>` -> [[x, y], ...] (a LIST — there may be several instances);
  `<entity>_size` -> [[width, height], ...]; `<entity>_velocity` -> per-tick
  position deltas [[dx, dy], ...].
- plus `score` [n], `won`, `lost`.
- objects may APPEAR or DISAPPEAR between ticks (entering/leaving the
  observable world) — handle absent keys/instances gracefully.

An OBJECT API is PROVIDED in your namespace — prefer it over raw coordinate math.
It is ALREADY IN SCOPE: call `objects(state)` directly. Do NOT import it. There is
no module to import it from, and `from objects import objects` inside `policy`
compiles and leaves `policy` callable, so nothing looks wrong until it runs — then
every tick raises ModuleNotFoundError, the runner substitutes its fallback action,
and the whole episode is lost. `import math` is fine; nothing else is needed.

__OBJAPI__

HOW THE RUNNER EXECUTES WHAT YOU RETURN — mechanism, so you can reason about it:
- Every tick it calls `policy(state, params)` with the CURRENT REAL state and the
  current parameter vector, and plays the action you return. No plan, no horizon.
- Between episodes, random search perturbs `PARAMS` in both directions, plays real
  episodes with each perturbation, and steps toward whatever scored better. Only
  the NUMBERS move. Your code is never edited by the search.
- If `policy` raises, or returns something not in the primitive action list, the
  runner takes a fallback action and records the failure. A policy that crashes on
  an unusual state is worse than a crude one that never does.

DESIGN RULES, in order of importance:
* FEW PARAMETERS. Search cost grows with `len(PARAMS)`: every extra constant costs
  real episodes. A compact form with 4 good parameters beats a general form with
  20. This is the single biggest thing you control.
* Pick the state fields that MATTER and ignore the rest. Most fields do not deserve
  a parameter of their own.
* Structure beats generality. Proportional and derivative terms on a key quantity,
  threshold and sign logic, a small number of explicit cases — these are cheap to
  fit. A dense weighted sum over every field is the expensive option; choose it
  only if nothing else fits the data.
* Anticipate. There is no lookahead, so a policy that acts on where things ARE acts
  too late. Velocity fields, or differences between successive positions, are how
  you act on where they are GOING.
* Make every parameter EARN its place. A constant the search drives to zero was
  wasted budget; a constant it pins at an extreme means the form is too weak there.
  You will be shown the fitted values — use them to revise the FORM next round.

failed. Read the ticks immediately BEFORE a death: that is where the form chose
wrong. And read the fitted parameter values as evidence about the form itself, the
same way a physicist reads a fitted coefficient.
